# Location-factor experiment: executive summary

Generated by `scripts/summarise_location_factor.py` from the JSON behind the three reports; every figure is read from them, so do not edit by hand. Start here, then read the reports listed under *Where things are*. All findings are ecological: they compare regions' published totals and follow no dwelling, provider or participant.

## The question

The NDIS pays an SDA provider a base amount (set by building type, design category and features) times a **location factor** set per SA4 and building type. The factor is the same across every SA3 in an SA4, but land costs are not, so the margin is highest on the SA4's cheapest land. **Hypothesis:** providers therefore build new SDA in the cheapest SA3s within each SA4, more than ordinary housing does (a market developer's sale price rises with land value; SDA's payment does not).

## The answer so far

1. **New SDA is built where new housing is built.** The growth-corridor SA3s hold 29% of new SDA (Jun 2023–Jun 2026) against 28% of dwelling approvals, 12% of population, 13% of participants waiting for SDA and 7% of older SDA stock. *Solid, national.*
2. **Within an SA4, new SDA leans toward the cheaper land in Victoria, but NSW does not confirm it.** In Victoria, with Valuer-General vacant-land prices (2021–2023): general building is neutral (+0.02), new SDA leans cheaper net of approvals, need, population and area (regression -19.13 (± 8.08); multinomial -1.28 (± 0.50)), and pre-NDIS SDA stock does not lean (-0.09, p 0.673; new SDA -0.48, p 0.023). In NSW, with the Valuer General's land values for 88 of 92 SA3s (23 SA4s in the tests), that does not repeat: new SDA leans no more toward cheap land than general building does (+0.13, p 0.403), and the placebo does not separate (new SDA +0.09, pre-NDIS stock -0.04). Only the regression keeps Victoria's sign and size, per within-state SD of land price (NSW -3.15 (± 3.14), Victoria -3.62 (± 1.53); both states pooled -3.81 (± 1.82)), and in NSW it is within two standard errors of zero. *Suggestive in Victoria (12 SA4s, inner Melbourne missing); not replicated in NSW.*
3. **Nationally the question cannot be settled with the Census cost measure.** SDA sits in cheaper SA3s than approvals (-0.18, p 0.015; regression -25.59 (± 12.30)), but the 2021 median mortgage reads new housing as dear, so general building appears to lean *dear* (+0.13), which inflates the gap. On that measure pre-NDIS stock leans cheap as much as new SDA does. In Victoria the same measure gives approvals +0.35 where land prices give +0.02: the mortgage measure is the problem, not the finding.
4. **No sign that the factor's level draws building.** Across 409 pairs of adjacent SA3s either side of an SA4 border, the higher-factor side gets no more SDA per approval, and with the post-2023 factors less (+36.35 (± 31.73) per log point with pre-2023 factors, p 0.267; -42.30 (± 18.23) with post-2023 factors, p 0.036), nor did the July 2023 re-set move building (-38.30 (± 28.05), p 0.193). With land prices as the cost control (165 pairs in NSW and Victoria) the higher-factor side still gets no more. The lean does not steepen where cost spreads wider (+11.14 (± 18.00) per SD; with land prices in both states, +1.09 (± 1.96)). This does not contradict point 2: factors largely track costs, so across a border the extra payment mostly buys dearer land.

**One-paragraph version:** New SDA is built where new housing is being built, concentrated in the growth corridors like other residential construction. In Victoria, where land prices can be measured directly, new SDA also leans toward the cheaper land within each region, beyond what general building, need or population explain, and older pre-NDIS stock does not — consistent with providers using a payment that is fixed across the SA4. NSW, measured with the Valuer General's land values, does not replicate that: there new SDA goes where general building, need and population go, and only a regression estimate, too imprecise on its own, points the Victorian way. So the lean toward cheap land is a Victorian finding, not yet a general one. There is no evidence that a higher factor, in itself, draws building across SA4 borders.

## What the data cannot carry

- **Two states for land cost, measured differently.** Victoria's are median sale prices of vacant lots; NSW's are the Valuer General's valuations of every residential parcel, per m². The pooled tests divide each state's relative land price by its own spread, which makes the two comparable in scale, not in kind. Queensland, South Australia and Western Australia publish no comparable free series.
- **Valuations, not sales (NSW).** A valuation is the Valuer General's estimate of unimproved land value at 1 July, for rating and land tax, not a price anyone paid.
- **SA3 publishes no building-type or category split of new build**, and a rise in enrolled HPS or Robust dwellings can include existing dwellings newly enrolled.
- **Per lot and per m² can disagree.** VGV's vacant land is home sites under 4,000 m², whose size still varies. NSW is measured per m², and per parcel instead the NSW rank test turns: new SDA leans toward *dearer* SA3s than approvals (+0.30, p 0.046).
- **2021 weights.** Locality prices reach SA3 through 2021 Census dwellings, before the SDA in question was built.
- **Timing.** Building takes a year or more, so part of what was enrolled after the July 2023 factor re-set was committed before it.
- **Why, not just where.** Cheap land is also available land; providers also weigh participants, services, transport and hospitals. Need is controlled; the rest is not.

## The pricing record

`data/pricing/` holds every SDA Pricing Arrangements version from 2021-22 v1.0 to 2026-27 v1.0 (13 in all). New-build location factors changed only in 2023-24 v1.0 and 2023-24 v1.4: until 30 June 2023 one table served all stock; from 1 July 2023 every new build uses a separate new-build table, unchanged since its January 2024 revision.
