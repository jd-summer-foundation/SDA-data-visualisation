# Longitudinal analysis: four questions

Generated by `scripts/analyse_panel.py` from `data/panel/panel.csv`. Every figure is computed; do not edit by hand. The panel is **region-level**: nothing here follows an individual dwelling or participant, and every flow is a net change in a region's published totals. See `VALIDATION.md` for which quarters support which measure.

## In brief

1. **Lasting, not a blip.** From Dec 2023 to Jun 2026 enrolled places grew by 9,503 and participants using SDA by 2,547. A new place brings about 0.24 participants into use, almost all within three quarters, and nothing after. Spare capacity grew from 7,481 to 14,437 places. Regions that stopped building did not drain: under 1% of their spare capacity was taken up in a year.
2. **The pipeline converts slowly, and much of it never.** A pipeline dwelling yields about 0.34 enrolments in the same category within a year, and pipeline in other categories predicts none. The March 2026 36-month rule removed at least 1,723 dwellings, and about 2,658 at the prior year's rate of new listings: 20%–31% of the pipeline.
3. **Waiting falls where stock is added, but slowly.** In the third of regions adding the most places, participants eligible but not yet using SDA fell 17%; in the third adding the least, 1%. Regions with more waiting than spare places went from 60 to 31, while spare places beyond local waiting rose from 1,192 to 6,497.
4. **Persistent, not noisy.** Of 304 region × category cells with enough participants, 16 move back and forth across a band in the 7 quarters from Dec 2024 to Jun 2026. The rest stay put or drift one way. High Physical Support rose from 1.81 to 2.58 places per participant nationally; Fully Accessible fell from 0.61 to 0.53.

## 1. Lease-up blip or lasting surplus?

**What the data can support.** Enrolled places by region for 13 quarters and participants with SDA in use for 11, so the change in use can be set against the change in places, region by region. **What it cannot.** Participants in use are not published by design category, so absorption is measured for a region's stock as a whole, not for High Physical Support or Robust separately. Places not in SDA use is an upper bound on vacancy: a place can be occupied by someone not funded for SDA.

### Absorption

Nationally, 9,503 places were added from Dec 2023 to Jun 2026 and 2,547 more participants came to use SDA: 27%. Spare capacity went from 7,481 to 14,437 (46% of places).

Region by region, the change in participants using SDA each quarter was regressed on the change in enrolled places that quarter and in earlier ones. The coefficients sum to the number of participants a new place brings into use:

Every specification lands between 0.19 and 0.30. The effect arrives within three quarters and the longer windows find no tail. Absorption is somewhat higher in small markets and where people were waiting, but nowhere near one for one.

Two plainer checks agree. **Regions that stopped building**, with places flat within 3% over a year (82 region-years in 34 regions), absorbed under 1% of their spare capacity in that year; it fell in 41 and rose in 37. If the spare were stock waiting to be leased up, it would drain once building stopped, and it does not. **Sharp bursts** (30 quarters with ≥15% growth, in 16 regions) look fully absorbed at first sight: in-use grew by 1.03 per burst place over the next year. But those regions were already adding 0.93 per burst place in a typical year, so the burst itself added about 0.10 per place.

### By design category

The places-to-need ratio **cannot register lease-up**: a participant who moves in stays in need, moving from eligible to in use. It can only fall back if need rises where stock is added. It does not. Across regions and categories, a new place adds 0.02 (± 0.01) to that category's recorded need, and 0.01 with uncategorised need spread pro-rata.

| Category | Places added, Dec 2024–Jun 2026 | Need added | New-build share | Ratio, need as recorded | Ratio, Missing pro-rata |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Improved Liveability | 571 | 655 | 47% | 0.66 → 0.68 (flat) | 0.49 → 0.55 (rising every quarter) |
| High Physical Support | 4,720 | 442 | 91% | 1.81 → 2.58 (rising every quarter) | 1.35 → 2.07 (rising every quarter) |
| Robust | 779 | 216 | 80% | 1.69 → 1.93 (up in 5 of 6 quarters) | 1.26 → 1.55 (up in 5 of 6 quarters) |
| Fully Accessible | 163 | 1,130 | 39% | 0.61 → 0.53 (falling every quarter) | 0.46 → 0.43 (down in 5 of 6 quarters) |

**Sensitivity to the uncategorised fifth.** Need without a design category fell from 25% to 20% of the total over these quarters. As it is classified, recorded need in each category rises, so ratios read as recorded drift down over time for that reason alone; High Physical Support and Robust rise despite it. The pro-rata reading removes the drift, and the direction is the same under both except for Improved Liveability, which improves only once the uncategorised need is spread.

**Verdict.** On the colleague's hypothesis: yes, some new stock is taken up in its first three quarters. But about 76% of it is not, spare capacity does not drain once building stops, and need does not rise to meet stock. The surplus is lasting. The new-build/vacancy link in the single-quarter vacancy view is therefore mostly oversupply, not lease-up.

## 2. Pipeline conversion

**What the data can support.** Pipeline dwellings and enrolled new-build dwellings by region and category, every quarter. **What it cannot.** Following a listed dwelling to enrolment. New enrolments between two quarters include dwellings listed after the first, so enrolments over the pipeline is not a conversion rate. The naive ratio over two years is 1.10 from Jun 2023, which is impossible as a rate. Two things can be measured instead.

**How fast, in the same category.** Per pipeline dwelling in a region and category, new-build enrolments in that category over the next year were 0.34 (± 0.04). Pipeline in the region's *other* categories predicts -0.01 (± 0.00), so there is no sign of net switching between categories. Individual switches that cancel out cannot be seen.

**How much never converts.** In the Mar 2026 quarter the pipeline fell by 1,723 dwellings more than were enrolled. That is a lower bound on removals that quarter, since it assumes nothing new was listed. Had new listings continued at the previous year's 936 a quarter, removals were about 2,658. The NDIA also removes enrolled dwellings it was slow to take out of the pipeline, and does not say how many of the removals were of that kind:

| Category | Pipeline before | Removed, lower bound | Removed, central | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Improved Liveability | 787 | 128 | 228 | 16%–29% |
| High Physical Support | 5,352 | 1,125 | 1,719 | 21%–32% |
| Robust | 1,645 | 396 | 580 | 24%–35% |
| Fully Accessible | 316 | 72 | 95 | 23%–30% |
| Multi-Design Category | 362 | 2 | 36 | 1%–10% |
| Total | 8,462 | 1,723 | 2,658 | 20%–31% |

A dwelling removed under the 36-month rule had been listed by March 2023, and was therefore already in the Jun 2023 pipeline of 3,544. If the removals were all of that kind, between 49% and 75% of that first cohort was dropped rather than enrolled. Either way, the NDIA's pipeline figures before March 2026 overstated what was coming by roughly 20%–31%.

**Calibrating the 'with pipeline' scenario.** The pipeline published now has had its stale listings removed, so its conversion should be better than the first cohort's. It should still be discounted:

- new-build enrolments each year run at about 34% of the pipeline in the same region and category at the start of that year;
- before the rule, 20%–31% of the listed pipeline was not going to enrol.

Counting one to two years of enrolment at that rate (roughly 34%–68% of today's pipeline), rather than all of it, is what the series supports.

## 3. Demand dynamics

**What the data can support.** Participants eligible but not yet using SDA, by SA4, for 11 quarters (Dec 2023–Jun 2026); no design category. **What it cannot.** Where waiting participants go when they move, or whether they are waiting for a specific category. A region's waiting count is where the participant lives, not where they would accept a place.

Waiting fell by 1,032 nationally, in 63 of 88 regions, and rose in 23. It fell where stock was added: rank correlation between places added and the change in waiting -0.54, and -0.60 in proportional terms across 83 regions.

| Regions by places added | Regions | Places added | In use added | Waiting change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Least third | 29 | 166 | 49 | -12 (-1%) |
| Middle third | 29 | 1,867 | 522 | -149 (-5%) |
| Most third | 30 | 7,470 | 1,985 | -864 (-17%) |

Per new place, waiting fell by 0.11 (± 0.03) and total recorded need rose by 0.12 (± 0.02). So about 48% of what a new place draws into use comes off the local waiting list, and the rest is new need recorded where the stock now exists: participants arriving or newly found eligible.

## Caveats that apply throughout

- **Region-level only.** No dwelling or participant is followed. Absorption, conversion and waiting changes are net changes in published totals.
- **Participants in use are estimated by the NDIA** from payments, bookings and address matching. Places not in SDA use is an upper bound on vacancy, not a count.
- **Places count '6+ residents' as six**, and the derivation carries the calibration error `VALIDATION.md` reports: 96–99% exact against P.7 each quarter.
- **Need by design category exists for seven quarters only**, and about a fifth has no category. Every category result above is given both as recorded and with that fifth spread pro-rata.
- **2024-25 Q3's participant figures are as at 2 April**, not 31 March.
- **Pipeline levels before and after March 2026 are not comparable.** Removal estimates assume removals before the rule were negligible; the NDIA says enrolled dwellings were sometimes left in the pipeline, so they may be slightly understated.
- **The regressions pool regions of very different sizes**, with standard errors clustered by region. They describe the typical relation, not any one region.
