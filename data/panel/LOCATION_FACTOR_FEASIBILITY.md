# Location-factor experiment, Phase 1: ingest and feasibility

Generated by `scripts/feasibility_location_factor.py` from committed files. Every figure is computed; do not edit by hand. Region-level throughout: nothing here follows an individual dwelling, and every SDA flow is a net change in a region's published totals. No test of the hypothesis is run here; this reports whether the data could support one.

## In brief

1. **The factors were re-set once in the window that matters.** Of the 13 versions from 2021-22 to 2026-27, new-build factors changed in 2: 2023-24 v1.0 and 2023-24 v1.4. Until 30 June 2023 one table served all stock; from 1 July 2023 new builds have their own, unchanged since. The 2026-27 factors used below are the ones in force since then.
2. **The factors vary a lot, and by building type.** Across the 88 SA4s the new-build factor for House, 2 residents runs 0.83–1.92. Within an SA4 the eleven building types differ by a median 0.15, and houses sit below apartments in 67 of 88 SA4s.
3. **Cost varies within SA4s, but modestly.** The within-SA4 standard deviation of log median mortgage is 0.10 (14% of all SA3 variance); the median SA4's cheapest and dearest SA3s differ by 0.22 log points (about 25%).
4. **Enough SA4s to test within.** 74 SA4s have at least two SA3s with cost data and at least 10 new-build dwellings added; they hold 96% of new-build growth.
5. **Cost is not collinear with the comparators.** Within SA4s, relative cost has a rank correlation of 0.17 with the SA3's share of approvals and 0.02 with its share of waiting participants; the controls explain 18% of relative cost. Approvals and need are collinear with each other (0.67), which matters for the regression, not the rank test.
6. **Many borders separate different factors.** Of 435 adjacent SA3 pairs in different SA4s (sharing at least 0.5 km of border), 268 straddle a new-build House, 2 residents factor gap of 0.05 or more, 171 of them with new SDA on both sides. But the pairs share SA3s and SA4 borders (189 distinct SA4 pairs), and adjacent SA3s across a border still differ in cost by a median 0.13 log points.

## 1. Pricing

**Sources.** `data/pricing/` holds the 2026-27 Pricing Arrangements for SDA (v1.0) and Pricing Schedule for SDA. `scripts/extract_pricing.py` reads both and requires them to agree on every location factor and every base amount; they do. It writes 2,024 location factors and 858 base amounts. Three things differ from what was expected:

- **Table numbers.** The location factors are Tables 23 and 24 (Appendices E and F) in the Arrangements, and Tables 35 and 36 in the Schedule. Tables are found by caption, not number.
- **Two retired SA4 names.** The existing-and-legacy table (not the new-build table) labels two regions by names the ABS retired in 2016: `QLD - Fitzroy` (now Central Queensland) and `QLD - Mackay` (now Mackay - Isaac - Whitsunday). These are mapped explicitly; any other unmatched name fails the build. All 88 SA4s are present in both tables.
- **A misleading heading.** The existing-stock tables *with* OOA head a column 'Robust No OOA'. The amounts are higher than the no-OOA table's, so the caption governs.

Base amounts are in Appendices A–D (post-2023 new build, pre-2023 new build, existing, legacy), by building type × design category, with variants for sprinklers, OOA, breakout room and (post-2023 new builds only) GST treatment. The shared-living amounts of Appendix H and the refurbishment costs of Appendix G are not base amounts and are not extracted.

### Across SA4 borders

Of the 435 usable pairs, 412 have new SDA on at least one side and 292 on both. Across pairs, the log gap in the national-mix factor has a rank correlation of 0.18 with the log gap in median mortgage (431 pairs): the higher-factor side is only slightly more likely to be the dearer one, so the factor gap is not just a cost gap. But the two sides are not alike in cost either (median gap 0.13 log points, as large as the within-SA4 spread), so the border comparison needs cost as a control, not as an assumption.

## Recommendation

Ahead of Phase 2:

1. **Within-SA4 rank test: run it.** 74 SA4s qualify at 10 dwellings, cost is not collinear with approvals or need within them, and the permutation within SA4 needs nothing else. It does not need past factors, since it asks only whether SDA leans toward cheap SA3s more than approvals do. The within-SA4 cost spread is narrow, so expect wide intervals.
2. **SA4 fixed-effects regression: run it, with need and approvals entered together and read for the cost coefficient only.** Their mutual collinearity makes their own coefficients unstable, but relative cost is well identified. HPS and Robust separately; Robust will be thin (see the counts above).
3. **Border test: run it on both factor sets, and on the change between them.** The pair count is large, but pairs overlap heavily, so the effective sample is nearer the 189 SA4 borders. Cluster by SA4 pair and control for the cost gap.
4. **Legacy placebo: run it, but expect little from it.** Legacy stock is only 151 dwellings, in 86 SA3s, at Jun 2026. It is a level, not a flow: its SA4 share is set against relative cost exactly as new build's is. Existing stock (3,670 dwellings in 243 SA3s) also predates NDIS pricing and would make a larger second placebo for where the building was, with the caveat that its *enrolment*, unlike its construction, can respond to price.
5. **Dose-response: run the cost-spread half; the factor-versus-cheapest-SA3 half needs a cost level comparable across SA4s, which a 2021 median mortgage only approximates.**
