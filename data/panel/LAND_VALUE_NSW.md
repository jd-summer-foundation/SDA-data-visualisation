# NSW: the within-SA4 tests with Valuer General land values

Generated by `scripts/analyse_land_value.py` from committed files. Every figure is computed; do not edit by hand. `LAND_VALUE_VIC.md` ran these tests for Victoria with vacant-land sale prices; this runs them for NSW with the NSW Valuer General's land values, then for both states together, then the dose-response and border tests with land price as the cost measure. Region-level and ecological throughout: it compares regions' published totals and follows no dwelling, provider or participant.

## The data

NSW Valuer General bulk land values, snapshot of 1 September 2026, supplied on request: a land value for every property in NSW at each 1 July base date from 2021 to 2025. © State of New South Wales through Valuer General NSW, licensed under [CC BY 3.0 AU](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/au/). **Changed from the original:** the property records are not published; `data/nsw_vg/land_value_by_locality.csv` holds only locality medians, and this report SA3 averages of them (`scripts/reduce_nsw_vg.py`).

These are **valuations, not sales**: the Valuer General's estimate of each parcel's land value, unimproved, at 1 July, made for rating and land tax. Unlike Victoria's sales they cover every parcel, so inner Sydney, which sells almost no vacant land, is measured too.

## The land measure

For each locality and base date, the median land value per m² of parcels zoned R1–R4 (general, low, medium and high density residential) of 100 to 3,999 m², strata schemes included (the Valuer General values a strata scheme once, for the whole site). A median needs at least 10 parcels. Each locality's mean log median over 1 July 2021–2023, to match Victoria's 2021–2023, is carried to SA3 by the locality's 2021 dwellings in each SA3 (ABS Mesh Blocks), and an SA3 is used when localities with a median hold at least 25% of its dwellings: 88 of 92 NSW SA3s, holding 99.8% of NSW's 2021 dwellings and 99.9% of its 953 new-build SDA dwellings. Left out: Blue Mountains - South, Illawarra Catchment Reserve, Lord Howe Island, Lower Murray.

Within SA4s (28 with two or more priced SA3s, against Victoria's 13), relative land price and relative mortgage agree in rank (+0.64; Victoria +0.66), and land spreads much wider: a within-SA4 standard deviation of 0.38 log points against 0.10 (Victoria, per lot: 0.19 against 0.08). Per m² separates small inner lots from large outer ones more than a per-lot price does. Growth corridors (3 of these SA3s) sit +0.01 log points from their SA4's mean on land price and +0.07 on mortgage.

## What it can and cannot support

- **General building in NSW leans dear on land** (+0.36, p 0.019); house approvals alone +0.10 (p 0.547). So the lean is other residential building (townhouses, units and apartments) going to the dearer SA3s, as density follows land value. Victoria's general building was neutral on land (+0.02).
- **Does NSW confirm that new SDA leans toward cheaper land?** No, not on its own. New SDA's share against relative land price is +0.18 (p 0.233) and its difference from approvals +0.13 (p 0.403), on 23 SA4s. Net of approvals, need, population and area the regression gives -8.19 (± 8.17) per log point and the multinomial -1.22 (± 0.74): the same sign as Victoria's, but within two standard errors of zero.
- **The placebo does not separate in NSW.** Against population, new build +0.09 (p 0.588); pre-NDIS stock -0.04 (p 0.816). In Victoria it did: new SDA -0.48, pre-NDIS stock -0.09.
- **Scale for scale, the regressions agree.** Per within-state SD of land price, NSW gives -3.15 (± 3.14) and Victoria -3.62 (± 1.53); within sampling error of each other: on this test NSW is too imprecise to confirm Victoria's estimate, not evidence against it. Pooled, -3.81 (± 1.82) (multinomial -0.22 (± 0.09)), beyond two standard errors. The pooled rank difference is +0.02 (p 0.860) and the pooled placebo gives new build -0.11 against pre-NDIS stock -0.05: in the pooled data only the regressions, which hold approvals, need, population and area fixed, point toward cheaper land.
- **Per parcel, NSW leans the other way.** On the per-parcel measure new SDA's difference from approvals is +0.30 (p 0.046): toward the *dearer* SA3s, beyond sampling error, while its regression is -11.44 (± 11.79). The rank tests compare shares alone; the regressions also hold each SA3's approvals, need, population and area fixed.
- **No dose-response on land either.** Pooled, the interaction is +1.09 (± 1.96) per SD of spread, within two standard errors of zero: the lean does not steepen where land prices spread wider.
- **Borders, with land as the control.** No factor-by-window combination shows building following the higher factor at p < 0.05. The land-price gap itself enters at -2.91 (± 1.67) share points per SD over the whole window (pre-2023 factors): the cheaper side of a border gets more SDA per approval, within two standard errors.
- **Limits.** Valuations are an estimate of each parcel's unimproved land value, not a price anyone paid. Per m² is not per lot: small inner lots carry the highest values per m², which is why the per-parcel measure is shown alongside. SA3 prices weight localities by their 2021 dwellings, before any of the SDA in question was built. Zones are as at the 2026 snapshot, and parcels created since 2021 carry no 2021 value. Every comparison is between regions.
